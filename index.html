During the hour

  • Open the HubSpot task list and work it top to bottom. Don't decide who to call during call time.
  • Every voicemail or text gets a new task 2 to 3 days out, with notes on the last touch.
  • Learn each contact's pattern. Some take four touches before you connect.
  • Use the gaps. 15 free minutes is 3 more attempts.
Clients and network

Referral Asks

Who to ask

  • Leaders and managers at your clients
  • Their salespeople. Shad's biggest account came from a salesperson's personal connection.
  • Your own network
  • Anybody. Drop the "who are they even connected to" head trash.
  • Keep a running list of who you asked and when.

At kickoff: set up the 3-month review

"Here's what's going to happen. About three months in, we're going to do a business review. Brian Daniels is going to get it scheduled with you. We'll go over what's been helping, where we could do better, and where else we could help. Then I'm going to ask you what makes a good referral for you, because I like to give referrals to my clients, and what makes a good referral for us."

At the review

  1. What's been helping so far? Get specifics.
  2. Where could we do better? Anything we missed or dropped?
  3. Where else could we help? Bring your own ideas.
  4. Referrals: who's a good referral for them, and what makes a good one for you.
  • Secure the account first, then ask.

The LinkedIn list ask

"Remember we talked about referrals? I've got an idea that's helped people with this. If I go through your LinkedIn and pull maybe 10 names, people you're connected to who might fit, and send that to you in a spreadsheet, would you be willing to take a look through it?"

"Take a week and look through it. I know a lot of us are connected to people on LinkedIn we don't know, so if you don't know them, no harm, no foul. If you know them well, would you be willing to make an intro? I can talk you through how to have that conversation."

  • Send 10 to 15 names.
  • "Know them well" means:
    • They'd pick up or call you back
    • They'd listen to your advice
  • Misses still pay off. Once they see the list, they know who you're looking for and often name someone better.
  • Offer the same: invite them to go through your LinkedIn for intros.
Build the list in Evaboot
  1. Save a search template once in Evaboot:
    • Current title: CEO, managing partner, managing director
    • Company revenue size
  2. Before a review, drop the client into the template to pull their connections that fit.
  3. Scan the matches in LinkedIn. Skip it if most are people you already work with.
  4. Export to a spreadsheet and clean it down to 10 to 15 names.
  5. Send it and give them a week.
  • Revenue data can be hit or miss.
  • Sales rep and BDR counts are new and unverified.
  • Also check who wrote them a LinkedIn recommendation, or got one from them. Those people usually know each other well.
Associations and events

Talks

When a client says "this would be great for my industry"

"Who in the industry?"

  • Listen for associations and boards they sit on.
  • Ask for an intro to whoever runs education or events there.
  • Shad's ASA talks started this way. A staffing client on an ASA board introduced him to the education director.
  • Every talk ask counts as an attempt. Log it in Prospecting Hour.

After a talk

  • Set meetings with the people who come up afterward.
  • If someone is ready to talk now, skip the short intro call:
    • Make the first meeting 45 minutes
    • Send the survey ahead
Daily and weekly tracking

Cookbook

Every day

  • Tap Copy for the cookbook when the hour is done.
  • Paste into today's cell in row 5 of this week's tab. It fills rows 5 to 14.
  • Note travel, conferences or vacation in row 3.

Every Monday

  1. Right-click last week's tab and choose Duplicate.
  2. Rename it Behavior Plan MMDDYY, using Monday's date. Example: Behavior Plan 102626.
  3. Change the date in B2.
  4. Clear last week's entries:
    • Monday through Friday, rows 5 to 14
    • Day notes in row 3
    • Talks Done and the closed-business numbers in the Monthly Radar
  5. Write the week's focus in row 2.
  • The Dashboard tab finds each week by its tab name, so the name has to match.

Every Friday

  • Open the Dashboard tab and check the weekly averages:
    • Prospecting attempts against 125
    • New meetings set against 10
    • New opportunities against 5
    • Meetings to opportunities against 50%
  • Rerun your close ratio in HubSpot and enter it.

Every month

  • In the Monthly Radar, fill in:
    • Events/Talks Done
    • Hiring Packages Closed
    • Sales Coaching/Consulting Closed
    • The recurring and one-time dollar columns
  • Log any recurring adds or drops on the Recurring Adds & Drops tab.

At year end

  • Rank your prospecting activities by the meetings and opportunities each one produced.
  • Do more of the top ones instead of adding something new. For Shad that's:
    • More business with current clients
    • Referrals from clients and his network
    • Talks
    • Webinars
What counts as a prospecting attempt
  • Picking up the phone
  • Asking anyone for a referral
  • Reaching out to get a talk
  • Talking to a current client about more ways to help

If it grows the business, it counts.
